# FINAL HCI PROJECT REPORT
## Smart Cafeteria Ordering Mobile Application

**Course:** Human–Computer Interaction (HCI) Group Term Assignment  
**Document Version:** 1.0 Final  
**Date:** Academic Term Submission  

---

### Executive Summary
This project presents the design, prototyping, user evaluation, and iterative refinement of the **Smart Cafeteria Ordering Mobile Application**, a smartphone solution designed to eliminate long lunchtime queues at university cafeterias. By enabling remote pre-ordering, meal customization, scheduled collection time slots, and express pickup tracking, the application reduces student and staff waiting times from an average of ~25 minutes to under 1 minute.

---

### Key Sections Summary

#### 1. User Research & Problem Framing
- Conducted contextual interviews with 5 campus participants (students and university staff).
- Identified major pain points: physical queue bottlenecking, lack of stock visibility, and time pressure between lectures.
- Developed 2 representative user personas (*Alex Chen - Student*, *Dr. Sarah Jenkins - Staff Member*) and mapped end-to-end user journeys.

#### 2. Information Architecture & Wireframing
- Designed 15 linked mobile screens in Balsamiq covering the entire user lifecycle.
- Structured bottom navigation around 5 core tabs: `Home | Menu | Orders | Favorites | Profile`.
- Created task flows for 3 primary interaction scenarios: *Place Order*, *Track Order Status*, and *Express Reorder*.

#### 3. Usability Evaluation & Iterative Refinement
- Tested Prototype Version 1 with 5 representative users across 5 standardized task scenarios.
- Identified critical friction points in Version 1 (hidden active order status, obscured collection time selector).
- Implemented Version 2 enhancements, increasing the System Usability Scale (SUS) score from **68.5 (V1)** to **86.5 (V2)** and achieving a 100% task completion rate.

#### 4. HCI Heuristics Compliance
- Verified compliance with Nielsen's 10 Usability Heuristics, emphasizing Visibility of System Status, Error Prevention, and Efficiency of Use.
